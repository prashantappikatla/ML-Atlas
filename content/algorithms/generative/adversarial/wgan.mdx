## Overview

Wasserstein [Generative Adversarial Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_adversarial_network) (WGANs) address the training instability and mode collapse issues prevalent in original Generative Adversarial Networks by replacing the Jensen-Shannon divergence with the Earth-Mover (Wasserstein-1) distance. This modification provides a smoother loss landscape and a meaningful metric that correlates with the quality of generated samples. WGANs achieve this by employing a critic network instead of a discriminator and enforcing a Lipschitz constraint on the critic.

## History

**2014** — [Ian Goodfellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Goodfellow) et al. introduce Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), establishing a framework for implicit density estimation through a minimax game between a generator and a discriminator.

**2017** — [Martin Arjovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasserstein_GAN), Soumith Chintala, and Léon Bottou publish "Wasserstein [GAN](/algorithms/generative/gan)," proposing the use of the Earth-Mover distance as a cost function to improve training stability and provide a meaningful loss metric.

**2017** — Ishaan Gulrajani et al. introduce "Improved Training of Wasserstein GANs" ([WGAN](/algorithms/generative/wgan)-GP), replacing weight clipping with a gradient penalty to enforce the Lipschitz constraint, leading to more stable and higher-quality results.

## Mathematical Intuition

### Earth-Mover Distance Definition

The Earth-Mover (or Wasserstein-1) distance, $W(\mathbb{P}_r, \mathbb{P}_g)$, between two probability distributions $\mathbb{P}_r$ (real data) and $\mathbb{P}_g$ (generated data) is intuitively the minimum "cost" of transforming one distribution into the other.
$$ W(\mathbb{P}_r, \mathbb{P}_g) = \inf_{\gamma \in \Pi(\mathbb{P}_r, \mathbb{P}_g)} \mathbb{E}_{(x, y) \sim \gamma} [||x - y||] $$
Here, $\Pi(\mathbb{P}_r, \mathbb{P}_g)$ is the set of all joint distributions $\gamma(x, y)$ whose marginals are $\mathbb{P}_r$ and $\mathbb{P}_g$ respectively. This distance provides a smoother gradient even when distributions are non-overlapping, unlike Jensen-Shannon divergence.

$$
W(\mathbb{P}_r, \mathbb{P}_g) = \inf_{\gamma \in \Pi(\mathbb{P}_r, \mathbb{P}_g)} \mathbb{E}_{(x, y) \sim \gamma} [||x - y||]
$$

### Kantorovich-Rubinstein Duality

Directly computing the Earth-Mover distance is intractable. The Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality allows us to express the Wasserstein-1 distance in a more tractable form:
$$ W(\mathbb{P}_r, \mathbb{P}_g) = \sup_{||f||_L \le 1} \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathbb{P}_r} [f(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)} [f(G(z))] $$
where $f$ is a 1-Lipschitz function, and $G(z)$ is the generator's output. In WGAN, the critic network $D$ is trained to approximate this 1-Lipschitz function $f$.

$$
W(\mathbb{P}_r, \mathbb{P}_g) = \sup_{||f||_L \le 1} \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathbb{P}_r} [f(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)} [f(G(z))]
$$

### WGAN Objective Function

The WGAN objective for the critic $D$ is to maximize the difference between the expected value of $D$ on real data and generated data:
$$ \max_D \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathbb{P}_r} [D(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)} [D(G(z))] $$
The generator $G$ is then trained to minimize this value, effectively trying to make the generated distribution $\mathbb{P}_g$ closer to the real distribution $\mathbb{P}_r$:
$$ \min_G \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)} [D(G(z))] $$
The critic's loss directly estimates the Wasserstein distance, providing a meaningful training progress indicator.

$$
\max_D \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathbb{P}_r} [D(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)} [D(G(z))]
$$

$$
\min_G \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)} [D(G(z))]
$$

### Enforcing Lipschitz Constraint

For the Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality to hold, the critic function $D$ must be 1-Lipschitz, meaning $||D(x_1) - D(x_2)|| \le ||x_1 - x_2||$ for all $x_1, x_2$. WGAN enforces this constraint by clipping the weights of the critic network to a small range, typically $[-c, c]$.
$$ w \leftarrow \text{clip}(w, -c, c) $$
This ensures that the gradients of $D$ with respect to its input are bounded, thereby implicitly enforcing the Lipschitz constraint.

$$
w \leftarrow \text{clip}(w, -c, c)
$$

## Algorithm Steps

1. Initialize generator $G$ and critic $D$ networks with random weights.
2. For each training iteration:
3.   For $n_{critic}$ steps:
4.     Sample a mini-batch of real data $x$ from $\mathbb{P}_r$.
5.     Sample a mini-batch of latent vectors $z$ from a prior distribution $p(z)$.
6.     Generate fake data $x_g = G(z)$.
7.     Compute the critic loss: $L_D = -(\mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathbb{P}_r} [D(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)} [D(G(z))])$.
8.     Update the critic's weights by taking an optimizer step to minimize $L_D$.
9.     Clip the critic's weights to a predefined range $[-c, c]$ after each update.
10.   Sample a new mini-batch of latent vectors $z$ from $p(z)$.
11.   Generate fake data $x_g = G(z)$.
12.   Compute the generator loss: $L_G = -\mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)} [D(G(z))]$.
13.   Update the generator's weights by taking an optimizer step to minimize $L_G$.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Type | Default | Range / Options | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `learning_rate_critic` | `float` | `0.00005` | positive float | The learning rate for the critic (discriminator) network's optimizer. |

| `epochs` | `int` | `100` | integer >= 1 | Total number of training epochs. |

## Strengths

- WGANs provide a more stable training process compared to traditional GANs, significantly reducing the problem of vanishing gradients.
- The critic's loss function directly estimates the Earth-Mover distance, offering a meaningful metric that correlates with the quality of generated samples.
- WGANs are less prone to mode collapse than original GANs, allowing the generator to produce a greater diversity of samples.
- The architecture is less sensitive to hyperparameter choices and network architecture, making it easier to train.
- The training objective is well-defined even when the real and generated data distributions are disjoint.

## Weaknesses

- Weight clipping, while enforcing the Lipschitz constraint, can lead to pathological behavior such as vanishing or exploding gradients if the clipping range is not chosen carefully.
- The choice of the clipping parameter $c$ is crucial and often requires extensive tuning, as an improper value can hinder learning or lead to instability.
- WGANs can still exhibit some degree of mode collapse, especially in complex datasets, although less frequently than standard GANs.
- Training can be slower due to the requirement of training the critic for multiple steps per generator update and the additional weight clipping operation.

## Use Cases

### Image Synthesis

Generating highly realistic and diverse images for various applications, from art to synthetic datasets.

### Data Augmentation

Creating synthetic data samples to expand limited datasets, improving the robustness of downstream machine learning models.

### Anomaly Detection

Learning the distribution of normal data and identifying samples that deviate significantly, useful in fraud detection or medical imaging.

### Image-to-Image Translation

Transforming images from one domain to another, such as converting satellite images to maps or sketches to photos.

### Drug Discovery

Generating novel molecular structures with desired properties for pharmaceutical research and development.
