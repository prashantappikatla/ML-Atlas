## Overview

[TransE](/algorithms/graph/transe) (Translating Embeddings) is a seminal knowledge graph embedding model that represents entities and relations as low-dimensional vectors in a continuous vector space. It models relations as translation operations between entity embeddings, aiming for $h + r \approx t$ when $(h, r, t)$ is a valid triple. This simple yet effective principle allows for efficient learning of relational patterns and prediction of missing links in knowledge graphs.

## History

**2013** — Antoine Bordes, Nicolas Usunier, Alberto Garcia-Duran, Jason Weston, and Oksana Yakhnenko introduce TransE, proposing the translation principle for knowledge graph embeddings in their paper 'Translating Embeddings for Modeling Multi-relational Data'.

**2014** — Zhen Wang, Jianwen Zhang, Jianlin Feng, and Zheng Chen propose TransH to address TransE's limitations with 1-N, N-1, and N-N relations by projecting entities onto relation-specific hyperplanes.

**2015** — Yankai Lin, Zhiyuan Liu, Maosong Sun, Yang Liu, and Xuan Zhu introduce TransR, which maps entities and relations into distinct entity and relation spaces, allowing for more expressive transformations.

**2015** — Théo Trouillon, Johannes Welbl, Sebastian Riedel, Éric Gaussier, and Michael Nickel introduce ComplEx, a complex-valued embedding model that can better capture symmetric and antisymmetric relations.

**2017** — Zhiqing Sun, Zhi-Hong Deng, Jian-Yun Nie, and Jian Tang introduce [RotatE](/algorithms/graph/rotate), which models relations as rotations in a complex vector space, effectively capturing various relational patterns including symmetry, antisymmetry, inversion, and composition.

## Mathematical Intuition

### Translation Principle for Relations

TransE models a relation $r$ as a translation vector from the head entity $h$ to the tail entity $t$. For a valid triple $(h, r, t)$, the embedding of the head entity $h$ plus the embedding of the relation $r$ should be approximately equal to the embedding of the tail entity $t$. This forms the core idea of the model.
$$ \mathbf{h} + \mathbf{r} \approx \mathbf{t} $$
where $\mathbf{h}, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t} \in \mathbb{R}^k$ are the $k$-dimensional vector embeddings for the head entity, relation, and tail entity, respectively.

$$
\mathbf{h} + \mathbf{r} \approx \mathbf{t}
$$

### Distance-Based Scoring Function

To quantify the plausibility of a triple $(h, r, t)$, TransE uses a distance-based scoring function. A smaller score indicates a more plausible triple. The score for a triple $(h, r, t)$ is typically defined as the $L_1$ or $L_2$ norm of the difference between $\mathbf{h} + \mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{t}$.
$$ f(h, r, t) = \| \mathbf{h} + \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{t} \|_{p} $$
where $p \in \{1, 2\}$ is the norm order. The $L_1$ norm is often preferred for its robustness.

$$
f(h, r, t) = \| \mathbf{h} + \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{t} \|_{p}
$$

### Margin-Based Ranking Loss

TransE is trained using a margin-based ranking loss function. The objective is to ensure that valid triples (positive samples) have lower scores than corrupted triples (negative samples) by a certain margin $\gamma$. A corrupted triple $(h', r', t')$ is generated by replacing either the head or tail entity of a valid triple with a random entity.
$$ \mathcal{L} = \sum_{(h,r,t) \in S} \sum_{(h',r',t') \in S'} \max(0, \gamma + f(h,r,t) - f(h',r',t')) $$
Here, $S$ is the set of valid triples, $S'$ is the set of corrupted triples, and $\gamma > 0$ is the margin hyperparameter.

$$
\mathcal{L} = \sum_{(h,r,t) \in S} \sum_{(h',r',t') \in S'} \max(0, \gamma + f(h,r,t) - f(h',r',t'))
$$

### Embedding Normalization Constraint

To prevent the embeddings from growing indefinitely and to ensure stability during training, the entity embeddings are typically normalized to have a unit $L_2$ norm after each update step. This constraint helps regularize the embedding space.
$$ \| \mathbf{e} \|_2 = 1 $$
for all entity embeddings $\mathbf{e}$. Relation embeddings are not typically normalized.

$$
\| \mathbf{e} \|_2 = 1
$$

## Algorithm Steps

1. Initialize entity and relation embeddings randomly, typically from a uniform distribution within a small range.
2. Normalize all entity embeddings to have unit $L_2$ norm.
3. For a specified number of training epochs, iterate through the following steps:
4. For each mini-batch of positive triples $(h, r, t)$ from the training set, generate a corresponding set of corrupted (negative) triples.
5. A negative triple $(h', r', t')$ is created by randomly replacing either the head entity $h$ with a random entity $h'$ or the tail entity $t$ with a random entity $t'$.
6. Calculate the score for each positive triple $f(h, r, t) = \| \mathbf{h} + \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{t} \|_{p}$ and each negative triple $f(h', r', t') = \| \mathbf{h'} + \mathbf{r'} - \mathbf{t'} \|_{p}$.
7. Compute the margin-based ranking loss: $\max(0, \gamma + f(h,r,t) - f(h',r',t'))$ for each positive-negative pair.
8. Update the entity and relation embeddings using an optimization algorithm (e.g., [Stochastic Gradient Descent](/algorithms/meta-learning/stochastic-gradient-descent) or [Adam](/algorithms/meta-learning/adam)) to minimize the total loss, and then re-normalize all entity embeddings to have unit $L_2$ norm.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Type | Default | Range / Options | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `embedding_dim` | `integer` | `50` | positive integer | The dimensionality of the entity and relation embedding vectors. |
| `learning_rate` | `float` | `0.01` | positive float | The step size used for updating embeddings during gradient descent optimization. |
| `margin` | `float` | `1.0` | positive float | The margin value $\gamma$ used in the margin-based ranking loss function. |
| `norm` | `integer` | `1` | 1 or 2 | The order of the norm ($L_1$ or $L_2$) used in the scoring function $f(h, r, t)$. |
| `batch_size` | `integer` | `1024` | positive integer | The number of triples processed in each mini-batch during training. |
| `n_epochs` | `integer` | `100` | positive integer | The total number of training iterations over the entire dataset. |
| `neg_sample_ratio` | `float` | `1.0` | positive float | The ratio of negative samples generated per positive sample during training. |

## Strengths

- TransE is conceptually simple and easy to implement, making it a good baseline for knowledge graph embedding tasks.
- It is computationally efficient compared to more complex models, especially for large knowledge graphs.
- TransE effectively captures 1-1 relations and can generalize well to unseen triples with these types of relations.
- The translation principle provides an intuitive geometric interpretation of relations in the embedding space.
- It can be easily extended and integrated into more complex knowledge graph reasoning systems.
- The model is robust to noise in the knowledge graph to some extent due to its margin-based loss function.

## Weaknesses

- TransE struggles with complex relation types such as 1-N, N-1, and N-N relations, where one entity can be related to multiple others via the same relation.
- It cannot effectively model symmetric relations, as $h+r \approx t$ implies $t+r \approx h$ only if $r \approx -r$, which is generally not true.
- The model assumes that entities and relations reside in the same vector space, which can limit its expressiveness for diverse relational patterns.
- Performance can be sensitive to the choice of hyperparameters, especially the margin $\gamma$ and the embedding dimension.
- It may not capture intricate logical patterns or higher-order relationships present in knowledge graphs.

## Use Cases

### Knowledge Graph Completion

Predicting missing links (head, relation, or tail) in an incomplete knowledge graph by inferring new valid triples.

### Link Prediction

Identifying new, previously unobserved relationships between entities, which can be crucial for expanding knowledge bases.

### Entity Resolution

Grouping different mentions that refer to the same real-world entity by comparing their learned embeddings to find semantic similarity.

### Question Answering

Enhancing knowledge-based question answering systems by leveraging learned entity and relation representations to find relevant answers.

### Recommendation Systems

Improving recommendations by modeling relationships between users, items, and their attributes within a knowledge graph structure.

### Information Retrieval

Enriching search results by understanding semantic relationships between query terms and entities in a knowledge base, leading to more relevant outcomes.
